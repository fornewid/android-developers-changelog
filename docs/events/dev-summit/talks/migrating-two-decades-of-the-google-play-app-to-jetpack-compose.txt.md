# Migrating two decades of the Google Play app to Jetpack Compose - Speakers: Kirill Grouchnikov (Software engineer), Jon Boekenoogen (Software Engineer) - Track: Apps foundation - Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7h_kcpNGKnw - Page: https://developer.android.com/events/dev-summit/talks/migrating-two-decades-of-the-google-play-app-to-jetpack-compose ## About the talk This year, Android officially became Compose-first for building all UI, placing legacy Views into maintenance mode. For Google Play, that journey began back in 2020. Across an engineering footprint spanning nearly two decades and billions of users, migrating to Jetpack Compose was achieved through proven best practices, close collaboration with the core Compose team and continuous tooling evolution. Discover how modernizing Google Play with Compose delivered measurable improvements in velocity and long-term stability. The session walks through connecting agentic Figma design workflows with Compose implementation, automating quality to prevent unnecessary recompositions and crafting expressive interfaces with dynamic color, rich typography and layered shapes. You will leave with tested strategies to triage bugs faster and scale your own Compose migration with confidence. ## Summary - Google Play moved 97% of user impressions to Compose-only screens over six years. Major pages also saw a 30% improvement in jank rate over the past 14 months. - Incremental adoption mitigates business risks across large codebases. Teams can build familiarity on low-traffic screens first, then advance toward hosting \`ComposeView\` directly at the Activity root. - Dynamic theming works entirely in code using \`MaterialTheme\` overlays and the Material Color Utilities library. This avoids Activity teardowns, though generating color schemes at build time helps limit runtime latency. - Unidirectional data flow and declarative UI simplify architecture and remove several types of race conditions. Running data sources and UI transforms in parallel improved Google Play's load latency by dozens of milliseconds. - Automated Lint checks enforce code quality and design system rules. Play uses them to block hardcoded colors and flag potential performance pitfalls such as \`BoxWithConstraints\`. ## Topics jetpack-compose, migration, performance, theming, unidirectional-data-flow,
