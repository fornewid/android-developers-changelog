# Outperform yourself in Compose - Speakers: Andrei Shikov (Software Engineer), Andrew Bailey (Software Engineer) - Track: Tools \& Performance - Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t5ujT_o8qj0 - Page: https://developer.android.com/events/dev-summit/talks/outperform-yourself-in-compose ## About the talk Jetpack Compose makes it easy to design high-quality user interfaces, but it can be difficult to track down issues that affect UI performance. These kinds of issues can lead to problems that your users notice, like startup latency, and dropped frames that appear as scroll jank or choppy animations. In this talk, we'll introduce tools to uncover what's impacting your UI's performance, focusing on Perfetto and (re-)composition tracing. We'll then explore specific anti-patterns you may be using that are secretly costing you, how you can find them in your code, and what positive patterns you can replace them with. By the end of this talk, you'll understand how to think in Compose with a performance mindset, and how to profile, analyze, and verify performance optimizations in your app. ## Summary - Perfetto traces record on-device activity and show Compose slices such as composition and layout along the main thread. Use them to inspect frame timelines and track phase durations. - Every operation inside composables has a cost, including launching and canceling coroutines. Audit side effect APIs and consider merging effects with identical keys to reduce composition overhead. - Composition tracing annotates composable functions, and recomposition tracing (1.13+) tracks state reads and writes causing recomposition. They help diagnose unexpected work directly within Perfetto traces. - ComputedState (Compose 1.13+) avoids unwanted invalidations and is typically faster than DerivedState during read-after-write invalidations. DerivedState remains useful when expensive calculations or allocations benefit from caching. - Skipping composables carries upkeep costs and parameter comparison overhead. Rely on strong skipping and object identity equality, or avoid invalidations entirely, rather than forcing stability. ## Skills - \[Android Skills repository\](https://github.com/android/skills) ##
